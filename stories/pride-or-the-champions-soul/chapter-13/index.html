Chapter 13
The Endless Night

The hushed luxury of the hotel offered Takeshi no comfort. He crossed the deserted lobby with a stiff step, his reflection, fragmented in the wall mirrors, sending back the image of a man whose dignity had been shaken. The night receptionist bowed slightly as he passed—a deference which, for the first time in his career, struck him as almost ironic.

In the lift, he loosened his tie with an abrupt gesture. The memory of that enigmatic smile pursued him, that quiet assurance which had defied his natural authority. She had sat down beside him with disconcerting familiarity, as if his reputation, his titles, everything he had built over the years, counted for nothing.

His room, for all its luxury, suddenly felt cramped. He took a few nervous steps to the picture window. Below, the lights of Tōkyō glittered, indifferent to his humiliation. The neon signs danced on the glass, creating shifting silhouettes that reminded him of the calculated grace with which she had handled the pieces, that elegance almost insulting in its simplicity.

The bed remained untouched. Takeshi settled into the armchair facing the city, his usually assured posture betrayed by fingers drumming on the armrest. The blue kimono filled his thoughts, the way it followed her movements, how it turned each of her gestures into a subtle choreography of domination.

The hours ticked by in a waltz of discomfort. The champion of Japan, used to imposing his own rhythm, found himself at the mercy of a stranger who had dared to challenge him in public, with an audacity that defied every convention. Worse still, she had forced him to concede defeat, not through technique, but through a patience that had exposed his own limits.

Dawn would come eventually, perhaps dispelling the thoughts that tormented him. But for now, in the half-light of this luxurious suite, Takeshi was discovering a vulnerability he had never suspected—that of a man whose certainties had been shaken by an opponent he should never have underestimated.
