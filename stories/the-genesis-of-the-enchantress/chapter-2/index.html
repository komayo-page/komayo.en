Chapter 2
The Cradle of Ōgi

Mayoko holds herself upright, her gaze anchored in that of her listeners. Her eyes reflect a nostalgic light, as if she were contemplating a distant time through the veil of the years.

My native village was a small, out-of-the-way place, she begins, her voice filled with a gentle melancholy. An only daughter, I was curious about everything, always in search of new discoveries. My parents encouraged this thirst for learning.

Her fingers delicately brush the bag lying at her side. When I was eight years old, my father gave me a gift that was to change my life. With precise, respectful gestures, she takes out an old game board and places it in front of her as if it were a precious treasure.

It was a makruk set, a game my father had brought back from one of his trading journeys to Haka— She breaks off, correcting herself with a faint smile. To Fukuoka, forgive me. A Chinese merchant had given it to him, confiding that the game came from a distant kingdom in the south.

Her hands gently caress the time-worn board. My friends and I would spend hours exploring its possibilities, creating our own rules. On its sixty-four squares, we ended up setting out the pieces of shōgi, which everyone then played in their own way, keeping only a single gold general, at the king's side. And our imagination led us to give it a reverse side: once it reached the enemy camp, it turned over and became free. Her gaze lights up. That is how ōgi1 was born.

1. The term ōgi comes from the Japanese 王棋, literally the king's game.
